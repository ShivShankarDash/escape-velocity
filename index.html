Why only 2 gym days works: your 3 calisthenics days already train push, pull and core with bodyweight. The gym days add what calisthenics can't: heavy legs, heavy hinges and progressive load, which is what tells your body to keep muscle in a deficit.
Training phases
Fuel (starting estimates)
  • Calories: your maintenance is roughly 2,750-3,000 kcal with this much activity. Aim for 2,100-2,200 kcal/day (about 0.7-0.8 kg/week).
  • Adjust weekly: 7-day average dropped less than 0.4 kg two weeks in a row? Cut 150 kcal. Dropping over 1 kg/week? Add 150 kcal back.
  • Protein: 150-170 g/day (about 2 g per kg of goal weight). Eggs, chicken, fish, paneer, Greek yogurt/hung curd, dal + soya, whey if convenient.
  • Plate rule: half vegetables, a palm or two of protein, a fist of carbs. Most carbs around training.
  • Water 3 L, sleep 7+ hours. Poor sleep makes the deficit eat muscle.
Lifting rules
  • Progressive overload: beat last session by 1 rep or 2.5 kg. Write every set down.
  • RPE 7-8: finish most sets with 1-3 reps left. Never grind ugly reps.
  • Warm up: 5 min easy cardio, then 2-3 lighter sets before your first lift.
  • Form first: film your squat, deadlift and bench in week 1 and compare form against a trusted coach's demo.
  • Barbell and dumbbells only, no machine needed. No rack: goblet or front-rack squat from the floor. No bench: dumbbell floor press.
Measure monthly
Weight lies during recomposition, because muscle gained hides fat lost. On the 1st of each month also note waist (at navel), chest and arm measurements and a front/side photo in the same light. Waist going down while lifts go up = it's working.

Not medical advice: if you have any joint, heart or other health condition, check with a doctor before starting, and get a trainer to check your form on the big lifts.
